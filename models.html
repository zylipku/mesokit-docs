Models¶

mesokit ships three model families. Each is written once for any number of spatial dimensions and registered under one name per dimension, so the same code trains on allen_cahn_1d and on cahn_hilliard_2d. The Overview tab covers what every model has in common and how to choose; the other tabs describe one family each — the equations, what is learned, the time stepping, the interpretability artifact, and the shipped recipes.

Both Onsager families write a dissipative dynamics as the flow of a free energy through a mobility,

\[ \frac{\partial u}{\partial t} = -(M + W)\,\frac{\delta V_\theta}{\delta u}, \qquad M \succeq 0 \text{ symmetric},\quad W = -W^{\top}, \]

so the learned potential is a Lyapunov function of the learned dynamics whenever \(W = 0\): interpretability and stability come from the same structure. They differ in where the structure is imposed. The spectral family assumes \(M\) and \(W\) are translation-invariant (one complex number per Fourier mode) and steps the linear part of \(\delta V/\delta u\) implicitly, which makes it cheap and exact for the diffuse-interface equations the shipped datasets come from. The classical family treats the grid as a \(d\)-dimensional ODE with \(d = N_1 \cdots N_d\), lets \(M\) and \(W\) depend on the state and puts no restriction on \(V\), at the price that every network has the grid size as its input width. FNO is the structure-free baseline the two are compared against.

Common surface

Every model consumes a batch of single-channel fields \(u \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times N_1 \times \cdots \times N_d}\) on a periodic box and exposes forward(u, dt) (one step), rollout(u0, n_steps, dt), list_artifacts() and artifact(name) — the DynamicsModel protocol described in Extending. mesokit train --model <name> resolves the class, the trainer registered for it (LitTrainer for all of them) and the recipe under configs/train/<dyn>/<name>/; the step size dt is read from the dataset’s time grid unless the recipe sets it. The grid in the recipe (Nx, Ny, Lx, Ly, and for the classical family the in_dim / grid_shape of its blocks) must match the dataset — mesokit train does not infer it.

All families are single-channel. For the multi-channel datasets (llg_lattice_2d, peridynamics_fracture_2d) pick one channel through the dataset.channel key of the recipe; multi-channel models are on the roadmap.

Interpretability

The two Onsager families share the potential artifact (mesokit interpret extract --artifact potential): the learned \(V_\theta\) on a constant-field slice with its components, the Fourier multipliers of \(M\) and \(W\) when the operators are diagonal in Fourier space (always for the spectral family, for the circulant blocks of the classical one), and, on Allen–Cahn checkpoints, a scatter of the learned against the analytic potential over real snapshots. Details are in the family tabs.

Model

The Onsager principle writes a dissipative dynamics as the flow of a free energy through a mobility. Spectral OnsagerNet takes this literally,

\[ \frac{\partial u}{\partial t} = -(M + W)\,\frac{\delta V_\theta}{\delta u}, \]

with \(M \succeq 0\) symmetric (dissipative), \(W\) antisymmetric (reversible) and \(V_\theta\) a learnable potential, and assumes both operators are diagonal in Fourier space on the periodic box. With \(\hat u_{\mathbf k}\) the rfftn coefficients this is one complex ODE per mode,

\[ \frac{d\hat u_{\mathbf k}}{dt} = -\bigl(K^m_{\mathbf k} + K^w_{\mathbf k}\bigr)\, \widehat{\frac{\delta V_\theta}{\delta u}}_{\mathbf k}, \qquad K^m_{\mathbf k} = \mu_{\mathbf k}^2 \ge 0,\quad K^w_{\mathbf k} = i\,\omega_{\mathbf k} \in i\mathbb{R}, \]

where \(\mu\) and \(\omega\) are the learnable arrays Km_params / Kw_params of the spectral shape \((N_1, \ldots, N_{d-1}, N_d/2 + 1)\). The squared parameterisation keeps the mobility non-negative; the purely imaginary \(K^w\) is what an antisymmetric operator looks like mode by mode. A learned \(K^m\) that vanishes at \(\mathbf k = 0\) is a conserved dynamics (Cahn–Hilliard type); a constant \(K^m\) is a non-conserved one (Allen–Cahn type); a non-zero \(K^w\) is a reversible, wave-like part.

Time stepping. The potential supplies its spectral gradient split into a linear part and a remainder, \(\widehat{\delta V/\delta u} = \hat L\,\hat u + \hat N(\hat u)\), and the default sbdf1 stepper treats the linear part implicitly:

\[ \hat u^{n+1}_{\mathbf k} = \frac{\hat u^n_{\mathbf k} - \Delta t\,(K^m + K^w)_{\mathbf k}\,\hat N_{\mathbf k}(\hat u^n)} {1 + \Delta t\,(K^m + K^w)_{\mathbf k}\,\hat L_{\mathbf k}} . \]

Explicit euler, rk2, rk3 and rk4 steppers on the same right-hand side are available through step_method. Steps are taken in Fourier space and transformed back, so the physical-space rhs(u) and forward(u, dt) are what training and rollout see.

The three terms are exactly what the potential artifact plots as \(V_0\), \(V_1\), \(V_2\).

Physics check

Because the family contains the data-generating equation as a special case, its correctness can be checked without training. With identity_M: true (\(K^m \equiv 1\)), learn_W: false (\(K^w \equiv 0\)) and AllenCahnPotential, the model is \(u_t = \Delta u + (u - u^3)/\varepsilon^2\). On a resolved grid (128² with \(\varepsilon = 0.1\)) its RK4 rollout over one frame of allen_cahn_2d matches the Dedalus frames to a mean-squared error of \(10^{-13}\), about \(10^{-9}\) of the frame-to-frame change; the test suite asserts this. The recipe configs/train/allen_cahn_2d/spectral_onsagernet_2d/physics_check.yaml runs the same model through mesokit train at zero learning rate, so the reported loss is the one-step scheme error. On the 32² fdr grid the interface (\(\varepsilon = 0.1\)) is narrower than a cell and the residual is dominated by aliasing of \(u^3\) — the potentials evaluate the nonlinearity on the native grid while the data are de-aliased — so treat that grid as a smoke test, not a benchmark.

The potential artifact

artifact("potential") returns a PotentialLandscape: the potential on a constant-field slice \(u \equiv c\) (with the \(V_0 / V_1 / V_2\) components when the potential has them), the learned multipliers \(K^m\) and \(\operatorname{Im} K^w\) on the spectral grid, and — when the checkpoint was trained on an Allen–Cahn dataset — a scatter of the learned \(V_\theta\) against the analytic \(V\) over real snapshots with its Pearson correlation and linear fit. In 1D the multipliers are plotted against the mode index; in 2D and 3D as images over \((k_x, k_y)\) (the \(k_z = 0\) plane in 3D).

A potential is selected by nesting it under model.potential in the recipe; null means the polynomial default, and the coercive.yaml recipes are the headline configuration (sine final activations, v_truncate = 16). The potential’s grid_shape / domain must match the model’s Nx, Ny, Lx, Ly, which in turn must match the dataset.

Model

The classical OnsagerNet (Yu, Tian, E, Li 2021) applies the generalised Onsager principle to a finite-dimensional state. mesokit uses it on the grid directly: the field on \(N_1 \times \cdots \times N_d\) points is flattened to \(\mathbf x = \operatorname{vec}(u) \in \mathbb{R}^d\), \(d = N_1 \cdots N_d\), and evolved by

\[ \frac{d\mathbf x}{dt} = -\bigl(M_\theta(\mathbf x) + W_\theta(\mathbf x)\bigr)\,\nabla V_\theta(\mathbf x), \qquad M_\theta \succ 0 \text{ symmetric},\quad W_\theta = -W_\theta^{\top}. \]

Nothing is assumed about Fourier space: the operators may depend on the state and the potential sees the whole vector at once. This is the most general Onsager parameterisation in the toolkit, and the most expensive — every network has \(d\) as its input width, so the family is meant for modest grids (256 points in 1D, \(32^2\) to \(128^2\) in 2D).

Potential. CoercivePotential is

\[ V_\theta(\mathbf x) = \frac{\alpha}{2}\,\lVert\mathbf x\rVert^2 + \mathrm{MLP}_\theta(\mathbf x), \qquad \alpha = e^{\log\alpha} > 0, \]

a scalar MLP (three hidden layers of width 256, tanh, no output bias) plus a coercive quadratic that guarantees \(V \to +\infty\) as \(\lVert\mathbf x\rVert \to \infty\). Its gradient is taken by autograd inside the right-hand side, with a second-order graph during training so the loss back-propagates through \(\nabla V\).

Operators. \(M\) and \(W\) are modules with an apply(x, v) method that returns the matrix–vector product; the matrices themselves are never formed and their structure holds by construction, not by penalty:

The circulant blocks are convolutions on the periodic grid parameterised by their Fourier multipliers; they are the classical-OnsagerNet counterpart of the Spectral OnsagerNet’s \(K^m_{\mathbf k}\) / \(K^w_{\mathbf k}\), written for any dimension through the shared SpectralGrid. band_size initialises them as short-range kernels (a wrapped Gaussian of that many grid points for \(M\), a small constant on the lowest modes for \(W\)). The state-dependent blocks are the original OnsagerNet design; conservation: null gives a purely dissipative model.

Time stepping. The right-hand side is generic, so only the explicit steppers are available: euler (default) and rk4. With \(W = 0\) the exact dynamics satisfy \(\dot V = -\nabla V^{\top} M \nabla V \le 0\), so \(V_\theta\) is a Lyapunov function of the learned model; the test suite checks that one Euler step of the purely dissipative model lowers \(V_\theta\).

The potential artifact

artifact("potential") returns the same PotentialLandscape as the spectral family: \(V_\theta\) on the constant-field slice \(u \equiv c\) with its two components (\(V_0 = \tfrac{\alpha}{2}\lVert\mathbf x\rVert^2\), \(V_1 = \mathrm{MLP}_\theta\)), the Fourier multipliers of \(M\) and \(W\) when the corresponding block is circulant (omitted, with a note in the summary, when it is state-dependent), and the Allen–Cahn reference scatter when the checkpoint was trained on an Allen–Cahn dataset.

Recipes

configs/train/allen_cahn_1d/onsagernet_1d/{default,circulant,smoke}.yaml
configs/train/kdv_1d/onsagernet_1d/default.yaml

default.yaml is the original low-rank design (\(M\) of rank 8, \(W\) of rank 4, both from MLPs of the state); circulant.yaml swaps both for the convolution blocks; the KdV recipe keeps \(W\) and steps with rk4; the 2D recipes use a circulant \(M\) and no \(W\) because the potential MLP already holds \(16384 \times 256\) weights in its first layer on the 128² grid. The blocks are nested under model.potential, model.dissipation and model.conservation; their in_dim (or grid_shape for the circulant blocks) must equal the model’s \(N_1 \cdots N_d\) (or grid), which is checked at construction.

Model

FNODynamics wraps neuralop.models.FNO as a right-hand side, \(\partial_t u \approx \mathcal{G}_\theta(u)\), and steps it with explicit Euler (default) or RK4. An FNO layer lifts the field to hidden_channels channels, multiplies the lowest n_modes Fourier coefficients per axis by learned complex weights, adds a pointwise linear path and a nonlinearity; n_layers such layers are followed by a projection back to one channel. n_modes is an integer or one entry per axis; fno1d / fno2d / fno3d differ only in the number of spatial axes handed to neuralop. It is the black-box baseline the interpretable families are compared against.

The spectrum artifact

Declared, not yet implemented: per-mode error and response diagnostics are on the roadmap, and artifact("spectrum") raises NotImplementedError until then.
