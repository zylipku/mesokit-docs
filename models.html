Models¶

mesokit ships three model families. Each is written once for any number of spatial dimensions and registered under one name per dimension, so the same code trains on allen_cahn_1d and on cahn_hilliard_2d. This page covers what every model has in common and how to choose; the pages under it describe one family each — the equations, what is learned, the time stepping, the interpretability artifact, and the shipped recipes — and how to add a model of your own.

fno1d, fno2d, fno3d

Fourier Neural Operator

a black-box right-hand side \(\partial_t u \approx \mathcal{G}_\theta(u)\)

spectrum — response gain and leakage per mode, one-step error against persistence, conservation residual

Both Onsager families write a dissipative dynamics as the flow of a free energy through a mobility,

\[ \frac{\partial u}{\partial t} = -(M + W)\,\frac{\delta V_\theta}{\delta u}, \qquad M \succeq 0 \text{ symmetric},\quad W = -W^{\top}, \]

so the learned potential is a Lyapunov function of the learned dynamics whenever \(W = 0\): interpretability and stability come from the same structure. They differ in where the structure is imposed. The spectral family assumes \(M\) and \(W\) are translation-invariant (one complex number per Fourier mode) and steps the linear part of \(\delta V/\delta u\) implicitly, which makes it cheap and exact for the diffuse-interface equations the shipped datasets come from. The classical family treats the grid as a \(d\)-dimensional ODE with \(d = N_1 \cdots N_d\), lets \(M\) and \(W\) depend on the state and puts no restriction on \(V\), at the price that every network has the grid size as its input width. FNO is the structure-free baseline the two are compared against.

Common Surface¶

Every model consumes a batch of single-channel fields \(u \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times N_1 \times \cdots \times N_d}\) on a periodic box and exposes forward(u, dt) (one step), rollout(u0, n_steps, dt), list_artifacts() and artifact(name) — the DynamicsModel protocol described in Custom Models. mesokit train --model <name> resolves the class, the trainer registered for it (LitTrainer for all of them) and the recipe under configs/train/<dyn>/<name>/; the step size dt is read from the dataset’s time grid unless the recipe sets it, and the grid (Nx, Ny, Lx, Ly, and for the classical family the in_dim / grid_shape of its blocks) is filled from the dataset unless the recipe sets it. See Training for the recipe layout and the Python path.

All families are single-channel. For the multi-channel datasets (llg_lattice_2d, peridynamics_fracture_2d) pick one channel through the dataset.channel key of the recipe; multi-channel models are on the roadmap.

Interpretability¶

The two Onsager families share the potential artifact (mesokit interpret extract --artifact potential): the learned \(V_\theta\) on a constant-field slice with its components, the Fourier multipliers of \(M\) and \(W\) when the operators are diagonal in Fourier space (always for the spectral family, for the circulant blocks of the classical one), and, on Allen–Cahn checkpoints, a scatter of the learned against the analytic potential over real snapshots. How to read it is on The Potential Artifact; the family pages say what each family puts into it.

How to Use This Section¶

  • Understand a family — Spectral OnsagerNet, OnsagerNet, Fourier Neural Operator: equations, learnable parts, time stepping, physics checks, recipes.

  • Write your own — Custom Models: the DynamicsModel protocol, the two implementation patterns, and the spectral helpers that make one implementation serve 1D, 2D and 3D.

  • Train one — Training.
