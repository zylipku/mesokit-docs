# Spectral OnsagerNet

Registered as `spectral_onsagernet_1d`, `spectral_onsagernet_2d`,
`spectral_onsagernet_3d`. An Onsager-structured dynamics whose mobility and
reactive operators are diagonal in Fourier space, with a learnable
potential. The interpretable family for translation-invariant PDE data.

## Model

The Onsager principle writes a dissipative dynamics as the flow of a free
energy through a mobility. Spectral OnsagerNet takes this literally,

$$
\frac{\partial u}{\partial t} = -(M + W)\,\frac{\delta V_\theta}{\delta u},
$$

with $M \succeq 0$ symmetric (dissipative), $W$ antisymmetric (reversible)
and $V_\theta$ a learnable potential, and assumes both operators are
diagonal in Fourier space on the periodic box. With
$\hat u_{\mathbf k}$ the `rfftn` coefficients this is one complex ODE per
mode,

$$
\frac{d\hat u_{\mathbf k}}{dt} = -\bigl(K^m_{\mathbf k} + K^w_{\mathbf k}\bigr)\,
\widehat{\frac{\delta V_\theta}{\delta u}}_{\mathbf k},
\qquad
K^m_{\mathbf k} = \mu_{\mathbf k}^2 \ge 0,\quad
K^w_{\mathbf k} = i\,\omega_{\mathbf k} \in i\mathbb{R},
$$

where $\mu$ and $\omega$ are the learnable arrays `Km_params` / `Kw_params`
of the spectral shape $(N_1, \ldots, N_{d-1}, N_d/2 + 1)$. The squared
parameterisation keeps the mobility non-negative; the purely imaginary
$K^w$ is what an antisymmetric operator looks like mode by mode. A learned
$K^m$ that vanishes at $\mathbf k = 0$ is a conserved dynamics
(Cahn–Hilliard type); a constant $K^m$ is a non-conserved one (Allen–Cahn
type); a non-zero $K^w$ is a reversible, wave-like part.

## Time Stepping

The potential supplies its spectral gradient split into a linear part and
a remainder, $\widehat{\delta V/\delta u} = \hat L\,\hat u + \hat N(\hat u)$,
and the default `sbdf1` stepper treats the linear part implicitly:

$$
\hat u^{n+1}_{\mathbf k} = \frac{\hat u^n_{\mathbf k} - \Delta t\,(K^m + K^w)_{\mathbf k}\,\hat N_{\mathbf k}(\hat u^n)}
{1 + \Delta t\,(K^m + K^w)_{\mathbf k}\,\hat L_{\mathbf k}} .
$$

Explicit `euler`, `rk2`, `rk3` and `rk4` steppers on the same right-hand
side are available through `step_method`. Steps are taken in Fourier space
and transformed back, so the physical-space `rhs(u)` and `forward(u, dt)`
are what training and rollout see.

## Potentials

| Class | $V_\theta[u]$ | Learnable | Use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `PolynomialPotential` (default) | $\int \tfrac{a}{4}u^4 + \tfrac{c}{3}u^3 + \tfrac{b}{2}u^2 + d\,u\,dx$, $a = a_{\sqrt{}}^2$ | $a, b, c, d$ | starter; contains the Allen–Cahn double well at $a = 1$, $b = -1/\varepsilon^2$ |
| `AllenCahnPotential` | $\int \tfrac12 \lvert\nabla u\rvert^2 + \tfrac{u^4}{4\varepsilon^2} - \tfrac{u^2}{2\varepsilon^2}\,dx$ | nothing | physics check; reference law for the `potential` artifact |
| `CoerciveAutogradPotential` | $\int \tfrac{a}{2}u^2 + F_\theta(u)\,dx + \int \tfrac{b}{2}\lvert\nabla u\rvert^2\,dx + \lvert v_\theta(\hat u_{\text{low}})\rvert$, $a, b \ge 0$ | $a, b$, pointwise MLP $F_\theta$, complex MLP $v_\theta$ on the lowest `v_truncate` modes per axis (`v_truncate`$^d$ inputs) | the headline interpretable choice: pointwise, local and nonlocal energy read off separately |

All potentials share the `SpectralPotential` base, which owns the grid, the
wavenumbers and $\lvert\mathbf k\rvert^2$, so each is written once for 1D, 2D
and 3D; the gradients of the coercive potential are obtained by autograd
(pointwise term in physical space, nonlocal term directly in Fourier space),
the gradient-energy term in closed form. The three terms are exactly what
the `potential` artifact plots as $V_0$, $V_1$, $V_2$. Writing a potential
of your own is covered in [Custom Models](custom_model.md).

## Physics Check

Because the family contains the data-generating equation as a special case,
its correctness can be checked without training. With `identity_M: true`
($K^m \equiv 1$), `learn_W: false` ($K^w \equiv 0$) and `AllenCahnPotential`,
the model *is* $u_t = \Delta u + (u - u^3)/\varepsilon^2$. On a resolved grid
(128² with $\varepsilon = 0.1$) its RK4 rollout over one frame of
`allen_cahn_2d` matches the Dedalus frames to a mean-squared error of
$10^{-13}$, about $10^{-9}$ of the frame-to-frame change; the test suite
asserts this. The recipe
`configs/train/allen_cahn_2d/spectral_onsagernet_2d/physics_check.yaml`
runs the same model through `mesokit train` at zero learning rate, so the
reported loss is the one-step scheme error. On the 32² `fdr` grid the
interface ($\varepsilon = 0.1$) is narrower than a cell and the residual is
dominated by aliasing of $u^3$ — the potentials evaluate the nonlinearity
on the native grid while the data are de-aliased — so treat that grid as a
smoke test, not a benchmark.

## What the `potential` Artifact Shows

`artifact("potential")` returns a `PotentialLandscape` (described in
[The Potential Artifact](../interpretability/potential.md)). For this
family it holds: the potential on the constant-field slice $u \equiv c$
with the $V_0 / V_1 / V_2$ components when the potential has them; the
learned multipliers $K^m$ and $\operatorname{Im} K^w$ on the full spectral
grid, always present because the operators are Fourier multipliers by
construction; and, when the checkpoint was trained on an Allen–Cahn
dataset, the scatter of the learned $V_\theta$ against the analytic $V$.

## Recipes

```text
configs/train/allen_cahn_1d/spectral_onsagernet_1d/{default,coercive,smoke}.yaml
configs/train/kdv_1d/spectral_onsagernet_1d/default.yaml
configs/train/allen_cahn_2d/spectral_onsagernet_2d/{default,coercive,physics_check,smoke,physics_check_smoke}.yaml
configs/train/cahn_hilliard_2d/spectral_onsagernet_2d/default.yaml
configs/train/ising_2d/spectral_onsagernet_2d/default.yaml
```

A potential is selected by nesting it under `model.potential` in the recipe;
`null` means the polynomial default, and the `coercive.yaml` recipes are the
headline configuration (sine final activations, `v_truncate = 16`). The
potential's `grid_shape` / `domain` must match the model's `Nx`, `Ny`,
`Lx`, `Ly`, which in turn must match the dataset. The recipe format is
described in [Training Recipes](../training/recipes.md).
