# Models

`mesokit` ships three model families. Each is written once for any number
of spatial dimensions and registered under one name per dimension, so the
same code trains on `allen_cahn_1d` and on `cahn_hilliard_2d`. This page
covers what every model has in common and how to choose; the pages under
it describe one family each — the equations, what is learned, the time
stepping, the interpretability artifact, and the shipped recipes — and how
to add a model of your own.

## The Families

| Registered names | Family | Structure assumed | What is learned | Artifact |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `spectral_onsagernet_1d`, `_2d`, `_3d` | [Spectral OnsagerNet](models/spectral_onsagernet.md) | Onsager principle, $M$ and $W$ diagonal in Fourier space | a free energy $V_\theta[u]$, a mobility spectrum $K^m_{\mathbf k}$ and a reactive spectrum $K^w_{\mathbf k}$ | `potential` |
| `onsagernet_1d`, `_2d`, `_3d` | [OnsagerNet (classical)](models/onsagernet.md) | Onsager principle on the flattened field, $M(\mathbf x)$ SPD and $W(\mathbf x)$ antisymmetric, possibly state-dependent | a coercive free energy $V_\theta(\mathbf x)$, the operators $M_\theta$, $W_\theta$ | `potential` |
| `fno1d`, `fno2d`, `fno3d` | [Fourier Neural Operator](models/fno.md) | none | a black-box right-hand side $\partial_t u \approx \mathcal{G}_\theta(u)$ | `spectrum` (declared, not yet implemented) |

Both Onsager families write a dissipative dynamics as the flow of a free
energy through a mobility,

$$
\frac{\partial u}{\partial t} = -(M + W)\,\frac{\delta V_\theta}{\delta u},
\qquad M \succeq 0 \text{ symmetric},\quad W = -W^{\top},
$$

so the learned potential is a Lyapunov function of the learned dynamics
whenever $W = 0$: interpretability and stability come from the same
structure. They differ in *where* the structure is imposed. The spectral
family assumes $M$ and $W$ are translation-invariant (one complex number per
Fourier mode) and steps the linear part of $\delta V/\delta u$ implicitly,
which makes it cheap and exact for the diffuse-interface equations the
shipped datasets come from. The classical family treats the grid as a
$d$-dimensional ODE with $d = N_1 \cdots N_d$, lets $M$ and $W$ depend on
the state and puts no restriction on $V$, at the price that every network
has the grid size as its input width. FNO is the structure-free baseline
the two are compared against.

## Common Surface

Every model consumes a batch of **single-channel** fields
$u \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times N_1 \times \cdots \times N_d}$ on a periodic box
and exposes `forward(u, dt)` (one step), `rollout(u0, n_steps, dt)`,
`list_artifacts()` and `artifact(name)` — the `DynamicsModel` protocol
described in [Custom Models](models/custom_model.md). `mesokit train
--model <name>` resolves the class, the trainer registered for it
(`LitTrainer` for all of them) and the recipe under
`configs/train/<dyn>/<name>/`; the step size `dt` is read from the
dataset's time grid unless the recipe sets it. The grid in the recipe
(`Nx`, `Ny`, `Lx`, `Ly`, and for the classical family the `in_dim` /
`grid_shape` of its blocks) must match the dataset — `mesokit train` does
not infer it. See [Training](training.md) for the recipe layout and the
Python path.

All families are single-channel. For the multi-channel datasets
(`llg_lattice_2d`, `peridynamics_fracture_2d`) pick one channel through the
`dataset.channel` key of the recipe; multi-channel models are on the roadmap.

## Choosing a Model

| If you want to … | Use |
| --- | --- |
| A predictive baseline with no structural assumptions | `fno<n>d` |
| A free energy you can plot, a mobility spectrum, and a reversible / dissipative split, on a translation-invariant problem | `spectral_onsagernet_<n>d` with `CoerciveAutogradPotential` (`--config coercive`) |
| A quick, few-parameter Onsager fit to look at the learned multipliers | `spectral_onsagernet_<n>d` with the default `PolynomialPotential` |
| Onsager structure without assuming translation invariance: state-dependent mobility, a potential over the whole field, modest grids | `onsagernet_<n>d` (`--config default` for low-rank $M$, $W$; `--config circulant` for per-mode multipliers) |
| A reversible (Hamiltonian-like) dynamics such as KdV | either Onsager family with $W$ enabled; `onsagernet_1d` with `rk4` for the classical one |
| To verify the pipeline on data whose law you know | `spectral_onsagernet_<n>d` with `identity_M`, `learn_W: false`, `AllenCahnPotential` |
| A model none of the above covers | [Custom Models](models/custom_model.md) |

## Interpretability

The two Onsager families share the `potential` artifact
(`mesokit interpret extract --artifact potential`): the learned $V_\theta$
on a constant-field slice with its components, the Fourier multipliers of
$M$ and $W$ when the operators are diagonal in Fourier space (always for the
spectral family, for the circulant blocks of the classical one), and, on
Allen–Cahn checkpoints, a scatter of the learned against the analytic
potential over real snapshots. How to read it is on
[The Potential Artifact](interpretability/potential.md); the family pages
say what each family puts into it.
