# Models

`mesokit` ships three model families. Each is written once for any number
of spatial dimensions and registered under one name per dimension, so the
same code trains on `allen_cahn_1d` and on `cahn_hilliard_2d`. The
**Overview** tab covers what every model has in common and how to choose;
the other tabs describe one family each — the equations, what is learned,
the time stepping, the interpretability artifact, and the shipped recipes.

::::{tab-set}

:::{tab-item} Overview

### The families

| Registered names | Family | Structure assumed | What is learned | Artifact |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `spectral_onsagernet_1d`, `_2d`, `_3d` | Spectral OnsagerNet | Onsager principle, $M$ and $W$ diagonal in Fourier space | a free energy $V_\theta[u]$, a mobility spectrum $K^m_{\mathbf k}$ and a reactive spectrum $K^w_{\mathbf k}$ | `potential` |
| `onsagernet_1d`, `_2d`, `_3d` | OnsagerNet (classical) | Onsager principle on the flattened field, $M(\mathbf x)$ SPD and $W(\mathbf x)$ antisymmetric, possibly state-dependent | a coercive free energy $V_\theta(\mathbf x)$, the operators $M_\theta$, $W_\theta$ | `potential` |
| `fno1d`, `fno2d`, `fno3d` | Fourier Neural Operator | none | a black-box right-hand side $\partial_t u \approx \mathcal{G}_\theta(u)$ | `spectrum` (declared, not yet implemented) |

Both Onsager families write a dissipative dynamics as the flow of a free
energy through a mobility,

$$
\frac{\partial u}{\partial t} = -(M + W)\,\frac{\delta V_\theta}{\delta u},
\qquad M \succeq 0 \text{ symmetric},\quad W = -W^{\top},
$$

so the learned potential is a Lyapunov function of the learned dynamics
whenever $W = 0$: interpretability and stability come from the same
structure. They differ in *where* the structure is imposed. The spectral
family assumes $M$ and $W$ are translation-invariant (one complex number per
Fourier mode) and steps the linear part of $\delta V/\delta u$ implicitly,
which makes it cheap and exact for the diffuse-interface equations the
shipped datasets come from. The classical family treats the grid as a
$d$-dimensional ODE with $d = N_1 \cdots N_d$, lets $M$ and $W$ depend on
the state and puts no restriction on $V$, at the price that every network
has the grid size as its input width. FNO is the structure-free baseline
the two are compared against.

### Common surface

Every model consumes a batch of **single-channel** fields
$u \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times N_1 \times \cdots \times N_d}$ on a periodic box
and exposes `forward(u, dt)` (one step), `rollout(u0, n_steps, dt)`,
`list_artifacts()` and `artifact(name)` — the `DynamicsModel` protocol
described in [Extending](extending.md). `mesokit train --model <name>`
resolves the class, the trainer registered for it (`LitTrainer` for all of
them) and the recipe under `configs/train/<dyn>/<name>/`; the step size
`dt` is read from the dataset's time grid unless the recipe sets it. The
grid in the recipe (`Nx`, `Ny`, `Lx`, `Ly`, and for the classical family
the `in_dim` / `grid_shape` of its blocks) must match the dataset —
`mesokit train` does not infer it.

All families are single-channel. For the multi-channel datasets
(`llg_lattice_2d`, `peridynamics_fracture_2d`) pick one channel through the
`dataset.channel` key of the recipe; multi-channel models are on the roadmap.

### Choosing a model

| If you want to … | Use |
| --- | --- |
| A predictive baseline with no structural assumptions | `fno<n>d` |
| A free energy you can plot, a mobility spectrum, and a reversible / dissipative split, on a translation-invariant problem | `spectral_onsagernet_<n>d` with `CoerciveAutogradPotential` (`--config coercive`) |
| A quick, few-parameter Onsager fit to look at the learned multipliers | `spectral_onsagernet_<n>d` with the default `PolynomialPotential` |
| Onsager structure without assuming translation invariance: state-dependent mobility, a potential over the whole field, modest grids | `onsagernet_<n>d` (`--config default` for low-rank $M$, $W$; `--config circulant` for per-mode multipliers) |
| A reversible (Hamiltonian-like) dynamics such as KdV | either Onsager family with $W$ enabled; `onsagernet_1d` with `rk4` for the classical one |
| To verify the pipeline on data whose law you know | `spectral_onsagernet_<n>d` with `identity_M`, `learn_W: false`, `AllenCahnPotential` |

### Interpretability

The two Onsager families share the `potential` artifact
(`mesokit interpret extract --artifact potential`): the learned $V_\theta$
on a constant-field slice with its components, the Fourier multipliers of
$M$ and $W$ when the operators are diagonal in Fourier space (always for the
spectral family, for the circulant blocks of the classical one), and, on
Allen–Cahn checkpoints, a scatter of the learned against the analytic
potential over real snapshots. Details are in the family tabs.

:::

:::{tab-item} Spectral OnsagerNet

### Model

The Onsager principle writes a dissipative dynamics as the flow of a free
energy through a mobility. Spectral OnsagerNet takes this literally,

$$
\frac{\partial u}{\partial t} = -(M + W)\,\frac{\delta V_\theta}{\delta u},
$$

with $M \succeq 0$ symmetric (dissipative), $W$ antisymmetric (reversible)
and $V_\theta$ a learnable potential, and assumes both operators are
diagonal in Fourier space on the periodic box. With
$\hat u_{\mathbf k}$ the `rfftn` coefficients this is one complex ODE per
mode,

$$
\frac{d\hat u_{\mathbf k}}{dt} = -\bigl(K^m_{\mathbf k} + K^w_{\mathbf k}\bigr)\,
\widehat{\frac{\delta V_\theta}{\delta u}}_{\mathbf k},
\qquad
K^m_{\mathbf k} = \mu_{\mathbf k}^2 \ge 0,\quad
K^w_{\mathbf k} = i\,\omega_{\mathbf k} \in i\mathbb{R},
$$

where $\mu$ and $\omega$ are the learnable arrays `Km_params` / `Kw_params`
of the spectral shape $(N_1, \ldots, N_{d-1}, N_d/2 + 1)$. The squared
parameterisation keeps the mobility non-negative; the purely imaginary
$K^w$ is what an antisymmetric operator looks like mode by mode. A learned
$K^m$ that vanishes at $\mathbf k = 0$ is a conserved dynamics
(Cahn–Hilliard type); a constant $K^m$ is a non-conserved one (Allen–Cahn
type); a non-zero $K^w$ is a reversible, wave-like part.

**Time stepping.** The potential supplies its spectral gradient split into
a linear part and a remainder, $\widehat{\delta V/\delta u} = \hat L\,\hat u + \hat N(\hat u)$,
and the default `sbdf1` stepper treats the linear part implicitly:

$$
\hat u^{n+1}_{\mathbf k} = \frac{\hat u^n_{\mathbf k} - \Delta t\,(K^m + K^w)_{\mathbf k}\,\hat N_{\mathbf k}(\hat u^n)}
{1 + \Delta t\,(K^m + K^w)_{\mathbf k}\,\hat L_{\mathbf k}} .
$$

Explicit `euler`, `rk2`, `rk3` and `rk4` steppers on the same right-hand
side are available through `step_method`. Steps are taken in Fourier space
and transformed back, so the physical-space `rhs(u)` and `forward(u, dt)`
are what training and rollout see.

### Potentials

| Class | $V_\theta[u]$ | Learnable | Use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `PolynomialPotential` (default) | $\int \tfrac{a}{4}u^4 + \tfrac{c}{3}u^3 + \tfrac{b}{2}u^2 + d\,u\,dx$, $a = a_{\sqrt{}}^2$ | $a, b, c, d$ | starter; contains the Allen–Cahn double well at $a = 1$, $b = -1/\varepsilon^2$ |
| `AllenCahnPotential` | $\int \tfrac12 \lvert\nabla u\rvert^2 + \tfrac{u^4}{4\varepsilon^2} - \tfrac{u^2}{2\varepsilon^2}\,dx$ | nothing | physics check; reference law for the `potential` artifact |
| `CoerciveAutogradPotential` | $\int \tfrac{a}{2}u^2 + F_\theta(u)\,dx + \int \tfrac{b}{2}\lvert\nabla u\rvert^2\,dx + \lvert v_\theta(\hat u_{\text{low}})\rvert$, $a, b \ge 0$ | $a, b$, pointwise MLP $F_\theta$, complex MLP $v_\theta$ on the lowest `v_truncate` modes per axis (`v_truncate`$^d$ inputs) | the headline interpretable choice: pointwise, local and nonlocal energy read off separately |

All potentials share the `SpectralPotential` base, which owns the grid, the
wavenumbers and $\lvert\mathbf k\rvert^2$, so each is written once for 1D, 2D
and 3D; the gradients of the coercive potential are obtained by autograd
(pointwise term in physical space, nonlocal term directly in Fourier space),
the gradient-energy term in closed form. The three terms are exactly what
the `potential` artifact plots as $V_0$, $V_1$, $V_2$.

### Physics check

Because the family contains the data-generating equation as a special case,
its correctness can be checked without training. With `identity_M: true`
($K^m \equiv 1$), `learn_W: false` ($K^w \equiv 0$) and `AllenCahnPotential`,
the model *is* $u_t = \Delta u + (u - u^3)/\varepsilon^2$. On a resolved grid
(128² with $\varepsilon = 0.1$) its RK4 rollout over one frame of
`allen_cahn_2d` matches the Dedalus frames to a mean-squared error of
$10^{-13}$, about $10^{-9}$ of the frame-to-frame change; the test suite
asserts this. The recipe
`configs/train/allen_cahn_2d/spectral_onsagernet_2d/physics_check.yaml`
runs the same model through `mesokit train` at zero learning rate, so the
reported loss is the one-step scheme error. On the 32² `fdr` grid the
interface ($\varepsilon = 0.1$) is narrower than a cell and the residual is
dominated by aliasing of $u^3$ — the potentials evaluate the nonlinearity
on the native grid while the data are de-aliased — so treat that grid as a
smoke test, not a benchmark.

### The `potential` artifact

`artifact("potential")` returns a `PotentialLandscape`: the potential on a
constant-field slice $u \equiv c$ (with the $V_0 / V_1 / V_2$ components when
the potential has them), the learned multipliers $K^m$ and
$\operatorname{Im} K^w$ on the spectral grid, and — when the checkpoint was
trained on an Allen–Cahn dataset — a scatter of the learned $V_\theta$
against the analytic $V$ over real snapshots with its Pearson correlation
and linear fit. In 1D the multipliers are plotted against the mode index;
in 2D and 3D as images over $(k_x, k_y)$ (the $k_z = 0$ plane in 3D).

### Recipes

```text
configs/train/allen_cahn_1d/spectral_onsagernet_1d/{default,coercive,smoke}.yaml
configs/train/kdv_1d/spectral_onsagernet_1d/default.yaml
configs/train/allen_cahn_2d/spectral_onsagernet_2d/{default,coercive,physics_check,smoke,physics_check_smoke}.yaml
configs/train/cahn_hilliard_2d/spectral_onsagernet_2d/default.yaml
configs/train/ising_2d/spectral_onsagernet_2d/default.yaml
```

A potential is selected by nesting it under `model.potential` in the recipe;
`null` means the polynomial default, and the `coercive.yaml` recipes are the
headline configuration (sine final activations, `v_truncate = 16`). The
potential's `grid_shape` / `domain` must match the model's `Nx`, `Ny`,
`Lx`, `Ly`, which in turn must match the dataset.

:::{tab-item} OnsagerNet

### Model

The classical OnsagerNet (Yu, Tian, E, Li 2021) applies the generalised
Onsager principle to a finite-dimensional state. `mesokit` uses it on the
grid directly: the field on $N_1 \times \cdots \times N_d$ points is
flattened to $\mathbf x = \operatorname{vec}(u) \in \mathbb{R}^d$,
$d = N_1 \cdots N_d$, and evolved by

$$
\frac{d\mathbf x}{dt} = -\bigl(M_\theta(\mathbf x) + W_\theta(\mathbf x)\bigr)\,\nabla V_\theta(\mathbf x),
\qquad
M_\theta \succ 0 \text{ symmetric},\quad W_\theta = -W_\theta^{\top}.
$$

Nothing is assumed about Fourier space: the operators may depend on the
state and the potential sees the whole vector at once. This is the most
general Onsager parameterisation in the toolkit, and the most expensive —
every network has $d$ as its input width, so the family is meant for modest
grids (256 points in 1D, $32^2$ to $128^2$ in 2D).

**Potential.** `CoercivePotential` is

$$
V_\theta(\mathbf x) = \frac{\alpha}{2}\,\lVert\mathbf x\rVert^2 + \mathrm{MLP}_\theta(\mathbf x),
\qquad \alpha = e^{\log\alpha} > 0,
$$

a scalar MLP (three hidden layers of width 256, `tanh`, no output bias)
plus a coercive quadratic that guarantees $V \to +\infty$ as
$\lVert\mathbf x\rVert \to \infty$. Its gradient is taken by autograd inside
the right-hand side, with a second-order graph during training so the loss
back-propagates through $\nabla V$.

**Operators.** $M$ and $W$ are modules with an `apply(x, v)` method that
returns the matrix–vector product; the matrices themselves are never formed
and their structure holds by construction, not by penalty:

| Block | Definition | Guarantee | Cost per product |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `DiagonalDissipation` | $M(\mathbf x) = \operatorname{diag}\bigl(\beta + \operatorname{softplus}\,m_\theta(\mathbf x)\bigr)$ | SPD, entries $> \beta$ | $O(d)$ + one MLP |
| `LowRankDissipation` | $M(\mathbf x) = \beta I + B_\theta(\mathbf x)\,B_\theta(\mathbf x)^{\top}$, $B \in \mathbb{R}^{d\times r}$ | SPD, eigenvalues $\ge \beta$ | $O(d r)$ + one MLP |
| `LowRankConservation` | $W(\mathbf x) = B_\theta(\mathbf x)\,C^{\top} - C\,B_\theta(\mathbf x)^{\top}$, $C \in \mathbb{R}^{d\times r}$ learnable | antisymmetric | $O(d r)$ + one MLP |
| `CirculantDissipation` | $M v = \mathcal F^{-1}\bigl[\operatorname{softplus}(\theta_{\mathbf k})\,\mathcal F v\bigr] + \beta v$, $\theta$ even in $\mathbf k$ | SPD, translation-invariant, state-independent | $O(d \log d)$ |
| `CirculantConservation` | $W v = \mathcal F^{-1}\bigl[i\,\theta_{\mathbf k}\,\mathcal F v\bigr]$, $\theta$ odd in $\mathbf k$ (zero at $\mathbf k = 0$ and Nyquist) | antisymmetric, translation-invariant, state-independent | $O(d \log d)$ |

The circulant blocks are convolutions on the periodic grid parameterised by
their Fourier multipliers; they are the classical-OnsagerNet counterpart of
the Spectral OnsagerNet's $K^m_{\mathbf k}$ / $K^w_{\mathbf k}$, written for
any dimension through the shared `SpectralGrid`. `band_size` initialises
them as short-range kernels (a wrapped Gaussian of that many grid points for
$M$, a small constant on the lowest modes for $W$). The state-dependent
blocks are the original OnsagerNet design; `conservation: null` gives a
purely dissipative model.

**Time stepping.** The right-hand side is generic, so only the explicit
steppers are available: `euler` (default) and `rk4`. With $W = 0$ the exact
dynamics satisfy $\dot V = -\nabla V^{\top} M \nabla V \le 0$, so $V_\theta$
is a Lyapunov function of the learned model; the test suite checks that one
Euler step of the purely dissipative model lowers $V_\theta$.

### Relation to Spectral OnsagerNet

| | Spectral OnsagerNet | OnsagerNet |
| --- | --- | --- |
| State | Fourier modes $\hat u_{\mathbf k}$ | flattened field $\mathbf x \in \mathbb{R}^d$ |
| $M$, $W$ | one multiplier per mode, state-independent | any SPD / antisymmetric block, may depend on $\mathbf x$ |
| Potential | functional of $u$ with a spectral $\hat L$ / $\hat N$ split | any scalar network of $\mathbf x$ |
| Stepper | SBDF1 (implicit linear part), explicit RK | explicit Euler / RK4 |
| Cost | $O(d \log d)$ per step, parameters independent of $d$ | networks of input width $d$ |
| Best for | translation-invariant PDE data, large grids | small grids, state-dependent mobility, checking what the spectral assumptions buy |

A classical model with `CirculantDissipation` and `CirculantConservation`
is the spectral model's operator structure with a free potential; comparing
it against `spectral_onsagernet_<n>d` isolates the effect of the potential
parameterisation, and swapping in the low-rank blocks isolates the effect of
state dependence in $M$ and $W$.

### The `potential` artifact

`artifact("potential")` returns the same `PotentialLandscape` as the
spectral family: $V_\theta$ on the constant-field slice $u \equiv c$ with its
two components ($V_0 = \tfrac{\alpha}{2}\lVert\mathbf x\rVert^2$,
$V_1 = \mathrm{MLP}_\theta$), the Fourier multipliers of $M$ and $W$ when
the corresponding block is circulant (omitted, with a note in the summary,
when it is state-dependent), and the Allen–Cahn reference scatter when the
checkpoint was trained on an Allen–Cahn dataset.

### Recipes

```text
configs/train/allen_cahn_1d/onsagernet_1d/{default,circulant,smoke}.yaml
configs/train/kdv_1d/onsagernet_1d/default.yaml
configs/train/allen_cahn_2d/onsagernet_2d/{default,smoke}.yaml
```

`default.yaml` is the original low-rank design ($M$ of rank 8, $W$ of rank
4, both from MLPs of the state); `circulant.yaml` swaps both for the
convolution blocks; the KdV recipe keeps $W$ and steps with `rk4`; the 2D
recipes use a circulant $M$ and no $W$ because the potential MLP already
holds $16384 \times 256$ weights in its first layer on the 128² grid. The
blocks are nested under `model.potential`, `model.dissipation` and
`model.conservation`; their `in_dim` (or `grid_shape` for the circulant
blocks) must equal the model's $N_1 \cdots N_d$ (or grid), which is checked
at construction.

:::{tab-item} Fourier Neural Operator

### Model

`FNODynamics` wraps `neuralop.models.FNO` as a right-hand side,
$\partial_t u \approx \mathcal{G}_\theta(u)$, and steps it with explicit
Euler (default) or RK4. An FNO layer lifts the field to `hidden_channels`
channels, multiplies the lowest `n_modes` Fourier coefficients per axis by
learned complex weights, adds a pointwise linear path and a nonlinearity;
`n_layers` such layers are followed by a projection back to one channel.
`n_modes` is an integer or one entry per axis; `fno1d` / `fno2d` / `fno3d`
differ only in the number of spatial axes handed to neuralop. It is the
black-box baseline the interpretable families are compared against.

### The `spectrum` artifact

Declared, not yet implemented: per-mode error and response diagnostics are
on the roadmap, and `artifact("spectrum")` raises `NotImplementedError`
until then.

### Recipes

```text
configs/train/allen_cahn_1d/fno1d/default.yaml
configs/train/kdv_1d/fno1d/default.yaml
configs/train/allen_cahn_2d/fno2d/{default,smoke}.yaml
configs/train/cahn_hilliard_2d/fno2d/default.yaml
configs/train/ising_2d/fno2d/{default,smoke}.yaml
```
