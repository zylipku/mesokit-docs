cahn_hilliard_2d — Cahn–Hilliard equation¶

Model. The Cahn–Hilliard equation is the conserved (\(H^{-1}\)) gradient flow of the same Ginzburg–Landau free energy,

\[ \partial_t u = \Delta \mu, \qquad \mu = \frac{\delta F}{\delta u} = u^3 - u - \kappa\, \Delta u, \qquad F[u] = \int \Bigl[ \tfrac14 (u^2 - 1)^2 + \tfrac{\kappa}{2} |\nabla u|^2 \Bigr]\, d\mathbf{x}, \]

on the periodic rectangle \([0, L_x) \times [0, L_y)\). Because the flux is the gradient of a chemical potential, \(\int u\, d\mathbf{x}\) is exactly conserved and \(F\) decreases monotonically. A homogeneous mixture with \(|\bar u| < 1/\sqrt{3}\) (inside the spinodal, where \(f''(\bar u) = 3\bar u^2 - 1 < 0\)) is linearly unstable to perturbations with \(|\mathbf{k}|^2 < (1 - 3\bar u^2)/\kappa\) — for \(\bar u = 0\), \(|\mathbf k| < 1/\sqrt\kappa\) with the fastest-growing mode at \(|\mathbf{k}| = 1/\sqrt{2\kappa}\): this is spinodal decomposition. The resulting bicontinuous domain pattern then coarsens by Lifshitz–Slyozov–Wagner evaporation–condensation with characteristic length \(L(t) \sim t^{1/3}\). The trajectories cover the quench, the decomposition, and the onset of coarsening.

This is the two-dimensional, single-species prototype for the conserved mesoscopic models one obtains by coarse-graining diffusive molecular dynamics (binary mixtures, alloys) to a composition field, so it is the natural synthetic stand-in while a physical dataset of that kind is being prepared.

Numerical scheme (NumPy/SciPy, Fourier pseudo-spectral in space, SBDF2 in time).

  1. Spatial discretisation. The field lives on the Nx \(\times\) Ny equispaced grid \(x_i = i\,L_x/N_x\), \(y_j = j\,L_y/N_y\) and is represented by its real 2D FFT \(\hat u_{\mathbf k}\), \(\mathbf k = (k_x, k_y)\) with \(k_x \in (2\pi/L_x)\{-N_x/2, \ldots, N_x/2 - 1\}\) and \(k_y \in (2\pi/L_y)\{0, \ldots, N_y/2\}\) (the negative \(k_y\) half follows from reality). Every derivative is a multiplication: \(\Delta \mapsto -|\mathbf k|^2\), \(\Delta^2 \mapsto |\mathbf k|^4\).

  2. Semi-discrete system. Transforming \(\partial_t u = \Delta(u^3) - \Delta u - \kappa \Delta^2 u\) gives, per mode,

    \[ \frac{d\hat u_{\mathbf k}}{dt} = \Lambda(\mathbf k)\,\hat u_{\mathbf k} - |\mathbf k|^2\, \widehat{u^3}_{\mathbf k}, \qquad \Lambda(\mathbf k) = |\mathbf k|^2 - \kappa |\mathbf k|^4 , \]

    where \(\Lambda\) is the full linear symbol (the anti-diffusive \(-\Delta u\) and the stabilising \(-\kappa\Delta^2 u\) together) and the cubic term is the only nonlinearity.

  3. De-aliasing of the cubic term. \(\widehat{u^3}\) is computed by zero-padding \(\hat u\) to a grid dealias \(\times\) (Nx, Ny), transforming to physical space, cubing pointwise, transforming back and truncating to the original band. In this product the Nyquist modes \(k_x = -N_x/2\) and \(k_y = N_y/2\) are dropped so the band is symmetric (they remain in \(\hat u\) itself and evolve under the linear symbol). With dealias = 2 (the default) this is exact: the largest triad sum is \(3(N_x/2 - 1)\), which on the \(2N_x\) grid aliases to \(3(N_x/2-1) - 2N_x < -(N_x/2 - 1)\), outside the retained band. The \(2/3\) rule would not suffice — it is exact only for quadratic products. dealias = 1 disables padding (aliasing errors feed the highest modes).

  4. Time stepping. Let \(\hat N^n = \widehat{(u^n)^3}\) and \(\Delta t =\) dt. The first step is SBDF1,

    \[ \frac{\hat u^1 - \hat u^0}{\Delta t} = \Lambda\, \hat u^1 - |\mathbf k|^2 \hat N^0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \hat u^1 = \frac{\hat u^0 - \Delta t\,|\mathbf k|^2 \hat N^0}{1 - \Delta t\,\Lambda}, \]

    and every later step is SBDF2 with the linear term implicit and the cubic term extrapolated to second order,

    \[ \frac{3\hat u^{n+1} - 4\hat u^n + \hat u^{n-1}}{2\Delta t} = \Lambda\, \hat u^{n+1} - |\mathbf k|^2 \bigl(2\hat N^n - \hat N^{n-1}\bigr) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \hat u^{n+1} = \frac{4\hat u^n - \hat u^{n-1} - 2\Delta t\,|\mathbf k|^2 (2\hat N^n - \hat N^{n-1})}{3 - 2\Delta t\,\Lambda}. \]

    The maximum of \(\Lambda\) is \(1/(4\kappa)\) (at \(|\mathbf k|^2 = 1/(2\kappa)\)), so the SBDF1 denominator \(1 - \Delta t\Lambda\) is positive for every mode iff \(\Delta t < 4\kappa\) and the SBDF2 denominator \(3 - 2\Delta t\Lambda\) iff \(\Delta t < 6\kappa\); the simulator enforces the tighter \(\Delta t < 4\kappa\). The stiff \(\kappa|\mathbf k|^4\) term being implicit, the practical step limit comes from the explicit cubic term on the interface scale; the default \(\Delta t = 0.05\) with \(\Delta x = 0.5\), \(\kappa = 1\) ran the full \(4000\)-step default without blow-up or visible oscillation (observed, not asserted by a test). All n_traj trajectories are advanced as one batch array (n_traj, Nx, Ny).

  5. Exact mass conservation. At \(\mathbf k = 0\) both \(|\mathbf k|^2\) and \(\Lambda\) vanish, so \(\hat u_0^1 = \hat u_0^0\) and \(\hat u_0^{n+1} = (4\hat u_0^n - \hat u_0^{n-1})/3\), whence \(\hat u_0^n = \hat u_0^0\) for all \(n\): the mean \(\bar u\) is exact in the float64 solver (\(\sim 10^{-10}\) drift over the default run) and \(\sim 10^{-7}\) as read back from the float32 file. The free energy is not discretely monotone by construction (no stabilisation term is added); a monotone decrease was observed at the default step but is not asserted by a test.

  6. Output cadence. Frame \(0\) is the initial condition; frame \(j\) is the field after \(j \cdot\) save_every steps, so times \(= (0, \Delta t\,\texttt{save\_every}, \ldots)\) — \(101\) frames \(2.0\) time units apart with the defaults. Fields are stored on the un-padded grid in float32.

The default configuration takes about ten minutes on one CPU core and writes a ~210 MB file (32 × 101 × 128² float32); fdr.yaml runs in under a second.

Initial conditions. \(u(\mathbf{x}, 0) = \bar u + \sigma\, \xi(\mathbf{x})\) with \(\xi\) i.i.d. standard normal at every grid point, i.e. an instantaneous quench of a homogeneous mixture of mean composition u_mean with thermal noise of amplitude ic_amplitude. With u_mean = 0 the two phases occupy equal area; off-critical values give droplet morphologies.
