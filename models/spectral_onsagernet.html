Spectral OnsagerNet¶

Registered as spectral_onsagernet_1d, spectral_onsagernet_2d, spectral_onsagernet_3d. An Onsager-structured dynamics whose mobility and reactive operators are diagonal in Fourier space, with a learnable potential. The interpretable family for translation-invariant PDE data.

Model¶

The Onsager principle writes a dissipative dynamics as the flow of a free energy through a mobility. Spectral OnsagerNet takes this literally,

\[ \frac{\partial u}{\partial t} = -(M + W)\,\frac{\delta V_\theta}{\delta u}, \]

with \(M \succeq 0\) symmetric (dissipative), \(W\) antisymmetric (reversible) and \(V_\theta\) a learnable potential, and assumes both operators are diagonal in Fourier space on the periodic box. With \(\hat u_{\mathbf k}\) the rfftn coefficients this is one complex ODE per mode,

\[ \frac{d\hat u_{\mathbf k}}{dt} = -\bigl(K^m_{\mathbf k} + K^w_{\mathbf k}\bigr)\, \widehat{\frac{\delta V_\theta}{\delta u}}_{\mathbf k}, \qquad K^m_{\mathbf k} = \mu_{\mathbf k}^2 \ge 0,\quad K^w_{\mathbf k} = i\,\omega_{\mathbf k} \in i\mathbb{R}, \]

where \(\mu\) and \(\omega\) are the learnable arrays Km_params / Kw_params of the spectral shape \((N_1, \ldots, N_{d-1}, N_d/2 + 1)\). The squared parameterisation keeps the mobility non-negative; the purely imaginary \(K^w\) is what an antisymmetric operator looks like mode by mode. A learned \(K^m\) that vanishes at \(\mathbf k = 0\) is a conserved dynamics (Cahn–Hilliard type); a constant \(K^m\) is a non-conserved one (Allen–Cahn type); a non-zero \(K^w\) is a reversible, wave-like part.

Time Stepping¶

The potential supplies its spectral gradient split into a linear part and a remainder, \(\widehat{\delta V/\delta u} = \hat L\,\hat u + \hat N(\hat u)\), and the default sbdf1 stepper treats the linear part implicitly:

\[ \hat u^{n+1}_{\mathbf k} = \frac{\hat u^n_{\mathbf k} - \Delta t\,(K^m + K^w)_{\mathbf k}\,\hat N_{\mathbf k}(\hat u^n)} {1 + \Delta t\,(K^m + K^w)_{\mathbf k}\,\hat L_{\mathbf k}} . \]

Explicit euler, rk2, rk3 and rk4 steppers on the same right-hand side are available through step_method. Steps are taken in Fourier space and transformed back, so the physical-space rhs(u) and forward(u, dt) are what training and rollout see.

All potentials share the SpectralPotential base, which owns the grid, the wavenumbers and \(\lvert\mathbf k\rvert^2\), so each is written once for 1D, 2D and 3D; the gradients of the coercive potential are obtained by autograd (pointwise term in physical space, nonlocal term directly in Fourier space), the gradient-energy term in closed form. The three terms are exactly what the potential artifact plots as \(V_0\), \(V_1\), \(V_2\). Writing a potential of your own is covered in Custom Models.

Every potential also accepts dealias (default None). The pointwise part of the gradient — \(u^3\) in the polynomial and Allen–Cahn potentials, \(F_\theta'(u)\) in the coercive one — is a product formed on the grid, which is exact only while the field is band-limited to a third of the spectrum; beyond that the high modes of the product fold back onto low ones. With dealias: 2 the field is zero-padded to a grid twice as fine along every axis, the nonlinearity is evaluated there and the result is projected back onto the symmetric band \(\lvert k_i\rvert \le N_i/2 - 1\) — the same convention as the de-aliased data generators, exact for a cubic (factor \(p\) is exact up to degree \(2p - 1\)). The gradient-energy and nonlocal terms are already spectral and are not padded, and no parameter or buffer changes, so checkpoints are unaffected. The cost is the pointwise evaluation on \(2^d\) times more points (about four times the model’s FFT work in 2D). Use it when the data are de-aliased and the grid is coarse relative to the interface width — the 32² fdr datasets — and leave it off when the data were produced without de-aliasing, since the model then has to reproduce the same aliased product. SpectralPotential.pointwise_to_spectral(fn, u_hat) is the helper a custom potential calls to get the same behaviour.

Physics Check¶

Because the family contains the data-generating equation as a special case, its correctness can be checked without training. With identity_M: true (\(K^m \equiv 1\)), learn_W: false (\(K^w \equiv 0\)) and AllenCahnPotential, the model is \(u_t = \Delta u + (u - u^3)/\varepsilon^2\). On a resolved grid (128² with \(\varepsilon = 0.1\)) its RK4 rollout over one frame of allen_cahn_2d matches the Dedalus frames to a mean-squared error of \(10^{-13}\), about \(10^{-9}\) of the frame-to-frame change; the test suite asserts this. The recipe configs/train/allen_cahn_2d/spectral_onsagernet_2d/physics_check.yaml runs the same model through mesokit train at zero learning rate, so the reported loss is the one-step scheme error. On the 32² fdr grid the interface (\(\varepsilon = 0.1\)) is narrower than a cell, and with the nonlinearity formed on the native grid the same RK4 rollout is off by a mean-squared error of \(2 \times 10^{-5}\) — 16% of the frame-to-frame change. That is not the resolution but aliasing of \(u^3\), which the Dedalus solver removes and the native product does not. With dealias: 2 (set in physics_check_smoke.yaml) the model forms \(u^3\) exactly as the data generator did and the residual drops to \(5 \times 10^{-14}\), \(3 \times 10^{-10}\) of the frame change — below the 128² figure — so the coarse grid is a faithful physics check too; the one-step sbdf1 loss the recipe reports is then the scheme’s own \(\Delta t\) error, about 100 times below the aliased value. The test suite asserts both residuals.

What the potential Artifact Shows¶

artifact("potential") returns a PotentialLandscape (described in The Potential Artifact). For this family it holds: the potential on the constant-field slice \(u \equiv c\) with the \(V_0 / V_1 / V_2\) components when the potential has them; the learned multipliers \(K^m\) and \(\operatorname{Im} K^w\) on the full spectral grid, always present because the operators are Fourier multipliers by construction; and, when the checkpoint was trained on an Allen–Cahn dataset, the scatter of the learned \(V_\theta\) against the analytic \(V\).

A potential is selected by nesting it under model.potential in the recipe; null means the polynomial default, and the coercive.yaml recipes are the headline configuration (sine final activations, v_truncate = 16). The potential’s grid_shape / domain must match the model’s Nx, Ny, Lx, Ly, which in turn must match the dataset. The recipe format is described in Training Recipes.
